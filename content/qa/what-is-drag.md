## Short Answer

Drag is an art form and performance tradition where performers exaggerate gender expression — through makeup, costume, character, and performance — for entertainment and artistry. It is a performance, not an identity, and it is distinct from being transgender.

## Drag Queens and Drag Kings

- **Drag queens**: Usually masculine or male performers presenting in exaggerated feminine characters. Some are gay men, some are trans, some are cis.
- **Drag kings**: Usually feminine or female performers presenting in exaggerated masculine characters.
- **Non-binary drag**: A growing category of performers who play with gender beyond the binary.

## Drag vs. Being Trans

Drag is a costume you put on for a show. Being trans is who you are, every day. A cis man doing drag is still cis; a trans woman doing drag is still a woman. The two communities overlap heavily — many famous trans women started in drag — but conflating them fuels harmful misconceptions.

## The History

- Drag performances date back centuries in theatre (Shakespeare's male actors played women).
- Modern queer drag emerged from working-class queer subcultures and Black and Latinx ballroom culture.
- RuPaul's Drag Race (2009–present) brought drag mainstream, generating a global fandom and a generation of professional drag artists.

## Drag Today

Drag shows are a cornerstone of queer nightlife — ranging from comedy and lip-sync to elaborate theatre and activism. Drag story hours (drag performers reading to children) have become a political target in recent years, making drag a symbol of queer visibility under attack. Supporting local drag is one of the most direct ways to support queer community.

## How to Support Drag Artists

- Attend shows and tip performers.
- Follow and promote local drag artists online.
- Fight back against anti-drag legislation and harassment.
- Respect their chosen names, pronouns, and characters — on and off stage.
