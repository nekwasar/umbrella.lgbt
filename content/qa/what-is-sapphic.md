## Short Answer

Sapphic describes women and non-binary people who love women — covering lesbians, bisexual women, pansexual women, and anyone whose attraction to women is central. The term comes from Sappho, the ancient Greek poet of Lesbos.

## Who Is Sapphic?

- Lesbians
- Bisexual women who love women
- Pansexual women
- Non-binary people who feel connected to womanhood and love women
- Queer women who don't use narrower labels

It's an umbrella — like "queer," it's inclusive by design, which is why many people prefer it.

## Why Use "Sapphic" Instead of "Lesbian"?

- **Inclusivity**: It includes bi, pan, and queer women who feel excluded from "lesbian" (which some define as exclusive attraction to women).
- **Avoids debates**: There are long arguments about who counts as a "lesbian." "Sapphic" sidesteps them.
- **Cultural marker**: Sapphic art, literature, and media are a distinct tradition — from Sappho's fragments to *Carol* to *Portrait of a Lady on Fire*.

## History

Sappho (circa 630 BCE) wrote love poetry to women on the island of Lesbos — the source of both "sapphic" and "lesbian." For over 2,000 years, her poetry has been central to women-loving-women culture.

## Sapphic Spaces and Culture

- Sapphic bars and clubs (increasingly rare but beloved — Henrietta Hudson in NYC, She Said in LA)
- Sapphic book clubs and online communities (r/sapphicbooks, r/actuallesbians)
- Sapphic film festivals (Dinah Shore, Provincetown, queer festivals worldwide)

If you love women and want a word that holds all of it — attraction, culture, community — "sapphic" is yours.
