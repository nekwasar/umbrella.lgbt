## Short Answer

Demisexual means experiencing sexual attraction only after forming a deep emotional bond. Demisexual people don't feel the "instant attraction" most people describe — but connection can unlock attraction over time.

## How It Works

- **No primary attraction**: Demisexual people don't feel sexual attraction based on looks, first impressions, or strangers. It's a very different experience from the norm.
- **Secondary attraction exists**: Once trust, intimacy, and emotional connection form, sexual attraction can develop — sometimes strongly.
- **The bond is individual**: "Deep" is defined by you — it might be months, years, or a specific kind of connection. There's no timer.

## Where It Sits

Demisexuality is part of the asexual spectrum (ace spectrum). It's related to but distinct from:

- **Asexual**: no (or very little) sexual attraction at all
- **Gray-asexual**: attraction that's rare or conditional — demisexuality is one specific condition

Many demisexual people use "gray-ace" as an additional label. Some don't use "asexual" at all — both choices are valid.

## Common Experiences

- "I never understood hookup culture"
- "People call me prudish or cold, but I just don't feel it with strangers"
- "My friends' celebrity crushes confuse me"
- "Attraction sneaks up on me after I've known someone a while"
- Thinking you're broken until you find the word

## Navigating Dating

- **Be upfront when you're ready**: "I need a real connection before attraction develops. It's not rejection — it's how I work."
- **You're not obligated to have sex to "test" attraction.**
- **Online dating can work** — take your time, build rapport, meet people repeatedly without pressure.
- **Community**: The ace spectrum community (AVEN, r/demisexuality) is full of people who get it.

## You're Not Broken

Demisexuality is a real orientation, not a problem to fix. "You just haven't met the right person" misses the point — meeting the right person is exactly how it works for you. The difference is that the connection comes first.
