## Short Answer

Queer sex education fills the gaps mainstream sex ed leaves: same-gender and trans-inclusive anatomy, consent, safer sex across body configurations, and pleasure that centers all bodies. If school never taught you this, you're not alone — it's here now.

## Why Queer Sex Ed Matters

- Most school sex ed is heteronormative — it literally doesn't cover how queer sex works.
- Misinformation harms: rates of STIs among gay and bi men are driven partly by gaps in education.
- Trans people deserve body-affirming sexual health information, not shame.

## Basics That Apply to Everyone

- **Consent**: Enthusiastic, ongoing, revocable. For every act, every time.
- **Safer sex basics**: Condoms and dental dams work across configurations. Lube reduces micro-tears (which increase STI risk) — use it.
- **STI testing**: Regular testing is normal, smart, and community-minded. Find free clinics — they exist in most cities.

## For Men Who Have Sex With Men

- **PrEP** prevents HIV (see our PrEP guide); PEP for emergencies within 72 hours.
- **Anal sex**: Start slow, use lube generously, stop if painful. Consider "topping/bottoming" conversations before sex, not during.
- **HIV status communication**: U=U — an undetectable partner cannot transmit. Status isn't a moral judgment.
- **Regular testing**: HIV, syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, and (for those who have anal sex) rectal and throat swabs — most clinics do this.

## For Women Who Have Sex With Women

- **Transmission is real but lower-risk**: STIs can pass between women — dental dams and shared-toy washing matter.
- **Oral sex**: Dental dams reduce risk; flavored ones exist.
- **Toys**: Clean between partners, use condoms on shared toys, and don't share toys with open cuts.
- **Pap smears and pelvic care**: Yes, you still need them, even if you've only had sex with women.

## For Trans People

- **Your body, your rules**: Sex doesn't require any specific body parts or configuration. You get to define what intimacy looks like.
- **Post-surgery care**: Follow your surgeon's healing timeline; intimacy after surgery is common, adjusted, and valid.
- **HRT and sex drive**: Hormones change libido for many people — normal and manageable, worth discussing with your provider.
- **Pregnancy risk is still real** depending on your configuration — don't assume. Ask your provider.

## Pleasure

Queer sex ed isn't just risk management — it's about joy. Understanding your body, communicating what feels good, and unlearning shame are all part of sexual health. There's no "normal" sex life. There's just yours.
