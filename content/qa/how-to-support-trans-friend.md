## Short Answer

Support a trans friend by believing them, using their name and pronouns, standing up for them, and educating yourself — not by making them your teacher.

## The Basics

- **Use their name and pronouns consistently**, even when they're not around.
- **Correct others gently**: "Actually, Alex goes by they/them."
- **Update your mental records**: Old photos, old stories, old jokes — adjust them in your head and in conversation.
- **Never out them**: Don't share their trans identity with anyone without explicit permission.

## Educate Yourself (Don't Make Them Teach)

Read, watch, and learn on your own time. If you don't understand something about being trans, Google first, ask them only the questions you genuinely can't answer yourself. Being the sole educator for your friend is exhausting for them.

## What Hurts

- Asking invasive questions about their body, surgery, or hormones.
- Calling their transition "a phase" or "brave" in ways that center your feelings.
- Introducing them with their deadname or old pronouns "because it's been so long."
- Making their transition about you ("I always knew," "this is so hard for me too").

## What Helps Most

- Treating them as exactly who they are — a friend with a full life, not a "trans friend."
- Being a calm, present ally in hostile rooms (family gatherings, work events).
- Checking in: "How are you doing this week?" goes a long way.
- If they're transitioning medically, being a low-pressure support person — offering rides, meals after surgery, or just company.

## If They Come Out to You

Thank them for trusting you. Tell them you love/support them. Ask what they need from you. Then do that thing. That's genuinely most of it.
