## Short Answer

Find queer-friendly housing by using LGBTQ+ housing networks, roommate matching services, community Facebook groups, and — for the safest experience — platforms built for queer community. Know your legal rights before you sign anything.

## Where to Look

- **LGBTQ+ Facebook groups and subreddits**: Search "[your city] LGBTQ+ housing" or "queer roommates" — community-run groups are where the real leads are.
- **Roommate apps with interest filters**: Roomi, Roomster, and SpareRoom let you filter for interests and community tags.
- **LGBTQ+ community center bulletin boards**: Physical and digital boards at community centers list verified queer housing.
- **College queer networks** (if you're a student): GSAs and LGBTQ+ resource centers often maintain housing lists.
- **Queer-focused housing co-ops**: Many cities have LGBTQ+ co-ops and house shares.

## Questions to Ask

- "Are you and your roommates queer?" (safety in numbers)
- "Is this a sober space?" / "Is there a strict visitor policy?"
- "Who lives here, and what's the vibe?"
- "Is the lease individual or joint?" (individual leases protect you if a roommate leaves)
- "Are trans people welcome here?" — ask directly if relevant to you.

## Know Your Rights

- **US**: The Fair Housing Act prohibits housing discrimination based on sex — and courts have ruled this covers sexual orientation and gender identity. Many states add explicit protections.
- **UK**: The Equality Act 2010 protects sexual orientation and gender reassignment in housing.
- **EU/Canada/Australia**: Anti-discrimination laws protect LGBTQ+ renters.
- Document everything: if you're denied housing and suspect discrimination, contact a fair housing organization.

## Safety Tips

- Never pay a deposit before viewing the property in person (or verified video call).
- Trust your gut — if a landlord or roommate makes you uncomfortable, walk away.
- For trans people: ask about bathroom situations, document issues, and know that deadnaming on leases is often avoidable (use legal name; chosen name can go on informal records).
