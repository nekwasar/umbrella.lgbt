## Short Answer

Use someone's pronouns by learning them, using them consistently, and correcting yourself without making it about you. If unsure, ask politely — "What pronouns do you use?" — and use whatever they tell you.

## The Basics

1. **Ask once, then use**: "What pronouns do you use?" is respectful and normal. If you already know, just use them.
2. **Don't assume by appearance**: Pronouns don't have a look. A butch person may use she/her; a feminine person may use he/him or they/them.
3. **Use them everywhere**: In conversation, introductions, email signatures, and behind the person's back. Consistency is respect.
4. **Grammar check**: "They" as a singular pronoun is grammatically correct — it's been standard English for centuries ("Someone left their umbrella").

## When You Slip Up

- Correct yourself immediately and briefly: "She — sorry, they — are coming."
- Don't over-apologize. A long apology makes the person manage your feelings.
- Don't explain, justify, or complain about grammar.
- Do better next time — practice mentally, use their name as a cue.

## When Others Mess Up

- Correct them gently if the person wants you to: "Alex goes by they."
- Don't do it loudly or embarrassingly unless the situation calls for it.
- Some people prefer to handle it themselves — follow their lead.

## Pronouns Are Not Optional

Refusing to use someone's pronouns isn't "an opinion" — it's harassment. Companies, schools, and platforms increasingly treat deliberate misgendering as discrimination. If you can't respect someone's pronouns, the respectful move is to remove yourself from their life, not to argue.

## Share Your Own

Sharing your pronouns in introductions, email signatures, and video calls normalizes the practice and makes spaces safer for everyone — especially for trans and non-binary people who can't always safely share theirs.
