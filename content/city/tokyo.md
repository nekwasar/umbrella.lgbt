## Safe Neighborhoods

- **Shinjuku Ni-chōme** — The world's densest gay district: hundreds of tiny bars, clubs, and venues packed into a few blocks. Tokyo's queer heart.
- **Asakusa** — Historic and slower-paced, home to the oldest gay bars.
- **Ueno / Okachimachi** — Traditional cruising and bar area.
- **Shibuya & Harajuku** — Trendy, youth-oriented, and queer-friendly for younger crowds.
- **Sugamo** — Known for lesbian bars.

## Queer Bars & Clubs

- **Arty Farty** (Ni-chōme) — The most famous gay bar in Japan.
- **The Annex** (Ni-chōme) — Popular, welcoming, multilingual.
- **Gods** (Ni-chōme) — Multi-floor classic.
- **Goldfinger** (Ni-chōme) — Legendary drag bar.
- **Aisotope Lounge** (Ni-chōme) — Women-focused queer bar.
- **Dragon Men** (Asakusa) — One of the oldest gay bars in Tokyo.
- **Star Pinot** (Sugamo) — Iconic lesbian bar.

## Community Centers

- **Ni-chōme community venues**: Monthly and weekly events, festival days, and community info booths.
- **Pride House Tokyo** — Support center for LGBTQ+ people, hosting events and counseling.
- **Tokyo Rainbow Pride (TRP) volunteer network** — Central organizing hub.
- **GRA (Gay Rights Assembly)** — Advocacy organization.
- **PAP (Partnership for Affirmation and Progress)** — HIV/AIDS community group.

## Healthcare

- **Shinjuku M's Clinic** — HIV/STI testing and treatment.
- **Tokyo Medical Center** — Gender clinic services.
- **SHIP** — Sexual Health and HIV Information Portal: English-language testing info.
- **PAP clinics** — Community HIV testing events.

## Events

- **Tokyo Rainbow Pride** — Japan's largest Pride (April), centered in Shibuya.
- **Ni-chōme Festival (Niji Matsuri)** — Summer street festival in the gay district.
- **Tokyo Lesbian Fest** — Annual lesbian gathering.
- **Pride House events** — Year-round community programming.

Note: Japanese queer life is often more discreet than in Western cities — bars are small, intimate, and reservation-based. Respect the culture's subtlety and you'll be welcomed warmly.
