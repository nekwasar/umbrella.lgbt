## Short Answer

PrEP is a medication that prevents HIV infection when taken consistently — a daily pill or bimonthly injection that reduces the risk of getting HIV from sex by about 99%.

## How It Works

PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis) puts anti-HIV medication in your body before exposure, so the virus can't establish an infection. It's like a contraceptive pill for HIV.

- **Daily pill**: Truvada or Descovy (generics available)
- **Injectable**: Apretude, taken every 2 months (increasingly popular)
- **On-demand ("2-1-1")**: Some regimens work for men who have sex with men — 2 pills before sex, 1 daily while exposed, 2 after.

## Who Should Consider It

- Men who have sex with men
- Trans women (particularly with HIV-positive partners)
- Anyone with a partner living with HIV
- Anyone who finds condoms inconsistent
- People who inject drugs

If you've had condomless sex with someone whose status you don't know, PrEP is worth asking your provider about.

## How to Get It

1. Ask any doctor or clinic — PrEP is a standard prescription.
2. Many LGBTQ+ clinics and Planned Parenthood offer it on informed consent.
3. US telehealth: Folx Health, Plume, Q Care Plus. UK: free via NHS sexual health clinics (Dean Street etc.).
4. Many countries and US states provide PrEP free or heavily subsidized.

## What It Doesn't Do

- Doesn't prevent other STIs (get regular STI testing)
- Doesn't prevent pregnancy
- Requires consistent use — skipping doses lowers protection
- Requires HIV testing every 3 months while on it

## Side Effects

Most people tolerate PrEP well. Mild side effects (nausea, headache) usually pass in the first weeks. Kidney monitoring via blood tests is standard.
