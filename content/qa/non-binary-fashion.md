## Short Answer

Non-binary fashion is whatever makes you feel like you — there are no rules, no required looks, and no one way to "look non-binary." The goal is self-expression that aligns your outside with your inside.

## Principles Over Rules

- **Comfort first**: If you don't feel good in it, it's not your look.
- **Dysphoria-aware dressing**: Dress to affirm your body or reduce discomfort — e.g., binders under looser tops, padding for curves, structured shoulders, or high-waisted pants — whatever serves you.
- **The fit is the transformation**: Tailoring changes everything. A well-fitted garment reads more intentionally than any label.

## Building a Wardrobe

- **Start with neutral basics**: Well-fitted tees, button-ups, trousers, and jackets in versatile colors. Neutral basics anchor any style.
- **Play with silhouettes**: Mix masculine and feminine cuts — a structured blazer over a flowing top; wide-leg pants with a fitted tee.
- **Accessories do the work**: Jewelry, belts, bags, shoes, and hair can shift a whole outfit's energy.
- **Thrift and swap**: Secondhand stores and clothing swaps are non-binary-friendly (no gendered departments dictating your choices) and budget-friendly.

## Practical Notes

- **Binders**: Never wear a binder larger than your size or for more than 8 hours — see our binding guide.
- **Packing**: If you pack or tuck, invest in purpose-made gear (STP packers, gaffs) — they're safer and more comfortable.
- **Gender-neutral stores**: Some brands specialize in inclusive fits — check their sizing charts, since cuts vary.
- **Shoes and bodywear**: Many "men's" and "women's" sizes differ; measure yourself and don't stress over the label on the tag.

## Resources

- r/NonBinaryFashion and similar communities for inspiration
- GC2B, Spectrum Outfitters for binders
- Local gender-affirming clothing exchanges (many community centers run them)

Remember: you're not dressing *for* anyone. Non-binary fashion is an ongoing conversation between you and your own reflection.
