## Short Answer

Queer people face higher rates of depression, anxiety, and suicide — driven mainly by stigma, rejection, and discrimination, not by being queer itself. Good mental health support exists, and it helps.

## Why Queer Mental Health Is Different

- LGBTQ+ people experience minority stress — the chronic, low-level stress of being a stigmatized minority.
- Family rejection is a major driver: LGBTQ+ youth with rejecting families are 8 times more likely to attempt suicide than those with accepting families.
- Access to affirming care is uneven: not all therapists understand queer and trans issues.

## Where to Find Help

- **Crisis lines**: The Trevor Project (LGBTQ+ youth, 24/7), Trans Lifeline (trans peer support), LGBT National Hotline.
- **Affirming therapists**: Use directories like TherapyDen, Inclusive Therapists, or Psychology Today filtered for LGBTQ+ specialties.
- **Community**: Support groups, pride centers, and online communities reduce isolation — a major factor in queer wellbeing.
- **Your body**: Regular movement, sleep, and time outside genuinely help — but they don't replace professional support.

## When It's an Emergency

If you're having thoughts of suicide: you are not a burden, and this feeling is temporary. Reach out — call or text the Trevor Project (US: 866-488-7386, text START to 678-678), Trans Lifeline (877-565-8860), or your local emergency line. In the UK: Samaritans 116 123. In many other countries, crisis lines exist too — the findahelpline.com directory lists them globally.
