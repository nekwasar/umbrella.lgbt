## Short Answer

There's no test for being asexual — if the label fits, it's yours. Common signs: never (or rarely) experiencing sexual attraction, not understanding what the fuss is about, and feeling "broken" until you find the word for how you feel.

## Common Signs

- **You've never felt the urge to sleep with anyone** — or felt it rarely
- **You've faked attraction**: "Oh yeah, they're hot" said to fit in
- **You didn't understand crushes**: Confusing aesthetic admiration, friendship, or wanting to be close for romantic attraction
- **You enjoy romance but skip the sexual part**: Dating feels performative until someone expects sex
- **"Broken"**: The most common ace experience — feeling defective, then realizing a whole community feels the same

## What Ace Isn't

- **Not the same as low libido**: Libido is a physical urge; asexuality is about *attraction*. Aces can have libidos.
- **Not the same as celibacy**: Celibacy is a choice; asexuality is orientation.
- **Not trauma or a disorder**: Being ace isn't caused by anything or in need of fixing.
- **Not "hasn't met the right person"**: That's like telling a gay person to wait for the right opposite-gender partner.

## The Ace Spectrum

- **Asexual**: No (or very little) sexual attraction
- **Demisexual**: Attraction only after deep emotional bonds
- **Gray-asexual**: Attraction that's rare, weak, or conditional
- **Aroace**: Both asexual and aromantic

## How to Explore

- Read ace community writing (AVEN — asexuality.org — is the hub)
- Talk to other aces online
- Try the label on in your head for a few weeks
- Remember: labels are tools. "Asexual" is yours if it makes your life more understandable — even if you're not sure yet.

## If You're Ace, You're Not Missing Out

Ace people build full, rich lives: deep friendships, romantic partnerships without sex, QPRs, chosen families, and communities. There's nothing to fix and nothing missing.
