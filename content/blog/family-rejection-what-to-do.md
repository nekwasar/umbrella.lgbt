# What to Do If Your Family Rejects You

Family rejection is one of the most devastating experiences a queer person can face — and one of the most common. The research is blunt: queer youth with rejecting families are 8 times more likely to attempt suicide than those with accepting families. But the other half of that research is just as important: queer people who build chosen family thrive. This guide is about surviving the rejection and building your life on the other side.

## Step 1: Protect Yourself First

Your safety outranks everything — including their feelings, including "keeping the peace."

- **Housing**: If there's any risk of being thrown out, secure a place to stay before any confrontation. LGBTQ+ youth shelters exist: Ali Forney Center (NYC), akt (UK), True Colors United (national US).
- **Money**: If you're financially dependent, start building independence now — a job, a bank account they can't access, a plan.
- **Documents**: Get copies of your ID, birth certificate, and medical records while you can.
- **Reach out today**: The Trevor Project (866-488-7386), Trans Lifeline (877-565-8860), or Switchboard (UK: 0800 0119 100). One call now can change everything.

## Step 2: Understand What's Happening

Rejection is rarely about you. It's about their beliefs, their fears, their culture, their religion, their inability to process something they were never taught to understand. None of that excuses cruelty — but understanding it protects you from internalizing the blame.

This is not your fault. You did nothing wrong by being honest about who you are.

## Step 3: Build Your Found Family

This is the queer tradition that has carried us for generations. Chosen family is real family:

- **Community centers**: Support groups, social events, volunteering — all lead to people
- **Online communities**: Discord servers, subreddits, and community platforms connect you to queer people everywhere
- **Mentors**: Older queer people who've been through this are walking proof that you survive it
- **Friends who become family**: Let the people who show up for you *in* — holidays, birthdays, everyday rituals

## Step 4: The Practical Survival Kit

- **Finances**: Look into independent living programs, queer-friendly employers, and community funds — many LGBTQ+ orgs run emergency financial assistance
- **Housing**: Roommate networks through community boards; queer-friendly housing groups on Facebook
- **Legal**: Some states/countries have emanticipation options for minors; youth orgs can help navigate
- **Health**: Community health centers offer sliding-scale care — don't skip checkups or mental health support

## Step 5: The Long Game

Two truths, held at once:

**Many families come around.** Rejection is often the first reaction, not the final one. Parents process, educate themselves, and sometimes come back — months or years later, changed. Leave the door open if you can, without letting them dictate the terms.

**Some never do.** And that's survivable. Millions of queer people live full, rich, loving lives without their birth families. The grief is real — grieve the family you deserved. Then build the one you have.

## What Never Helps

- Pretending you're straight to keep them
- Staying in a dangerous home "out of love"
- Accepting abuse as the price of contact
- Blaming yourself for their choices

## The Bottom Line

Family rejection tells you about them, not about you. Your worth was never conditional on their acceptance. There is a whole community — right now, everywhere — of people who will love you without conditions. Go find them. You are not alone, and you are not the first person to walk this road.

There is room under the umbrella. Always.
