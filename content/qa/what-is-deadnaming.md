## Short Answer

Deadnaming is calling a trans or non-binary person by the name they were given at birth and no longer use. It's harmful because it invalidates their identity, can trigger dysphoria, and can out them to people who shouldn't know.

## Why It Hurts

- **Invalidation**: Your chosen name is who you are. A deadname says "I don't recognize that."
- **Dysphoria**: Hearing the old name is often deeply distressing, like being misgendered.
- **Outing**: A deadname can reveal a person's trans identity to people who don't know — potentially dangerous in hostile environments.
- **Repetition compounds it**: A single slip is a mistake; repeated deadnaming is a pattern of disrespect.

## When It Happens by Accident

People genuinely slip up, especially with long-standing relationships. The right response:

1. Correct yourself quickly: "Sorry — Alex."
2. Move on. No long speeches, no making the person comfort you.
3. Get it right next time. Practice if you need to.

## When It's Intentional

Some people deadname deliberately to punish, control, or "prove a point." That's a form of abuse. You are not required to tolerate it. Set a boundary, enforce it, and remove yourself from people who weaponize your name.

## How to Be a Good Ally

- Update contacts, group chats, and written records to the chosen name.
- Correct others — even behind the person's back: "They go by Alex now."
- Never share someone's deadname with others. If you knew them before their transition, act like you've always known their chosen name.
- In documents and records, use their legal name where required — but use their chosen name everywhere it's your choice.
