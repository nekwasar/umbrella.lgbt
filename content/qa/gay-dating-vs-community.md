## The Problem

Almost every queer app on the market is either a dating app or a hookup app. Grindr, Scruff, Jack'd, Sniffies, Her, Taimi — even the ones that claim to be "community platforms" are still built around matching, swiping, and dating algorithms.

This creates two problems:
1. **Queer people who aren't looking for dates or hookups have nowhere to go.** This includes teenagers, people in relationships, asexual and aromantic people, and anyone who just wants friends.
2. **Dating apps are not safe spaces.** They're designed for rapid connections with strangers, not for building lasting community.

## What Exists Now

These are the closest alternatives, but none of them solve the problem:

- **Reddit** (r/lgbt, r/asktransgender, etc.) — Great for discussion but not a real community platform. No profiles, no local events, no safety features. Owned by a corporation.
- **Discord servers** — Good for small groups but terrible for discovery. You have to know someone to get in. No persistence, no SEO.
- **Lex** — Started as a text-based queer personals app. Tried to pivot to community. Users rebelled. Now acquired and uncertain.
- **TikTok / Instagram** — Algorithmic, corporate, actively suppresses queer content.

## What Umbrella.lgbt Is Doing Differently

Umbrella.lgbt is community-first, not dating-first. The app has four sections:

- **Community** — Groups organized around interests, identities, and locations. Events, discussions, real-life meetups.
- **Meet** — Browse queer people near you. Filter by proximity. No matching algorithms.
- **Q&A** — Public, searchable questions and answers. Indexed on Google so people find help.
- **Profiles** — Customizable, expressive. Like MySpace for a new generation.

Dating can happen organically through Meet and messages — but it's never the core of the platform.
