# Asexual and Aromantic: The A in LGBTQIA+

The "A" in LGBTQIA+ is the most misunderstood letter in the acronym. It stands for asexual, aromantic, and agender — identities that are often erased, dismissed, or assumed to mean something they don't. This guide explains what the A actually means.

## What Asexuality Is

An asexual person experiences little or no sexual attraction to others. That's the entire definition — and it's frequently misunderstood.

Asexuality is not:
- **Celibacy** (a choice — asexuality is orientation)
- **Low libido** (libido is a physical urge; asexuality is about attraction)
- **Trauma response** (ace people aren't "broken" or in need of fixing)
- **A phase** (for many, it's lifelong)
- **"Hasn't met the right person"** (that's like telling a gay person to wait for the right opposite-gender partner)

## What Aromanticism Is

An aromantic person experiences little or no *romantic* attraction. Sexual and romantic attraction are separate systems — a person can be:

- Asexual and aromantic ("aroace")
- Asexual but romantic (wants romance, not sex)
- Aromantic but sexual (wants sex, not romance)
- Neither, both, or anywhere in between

## The Spectrums

Both identities are spectrums, not switches:

- **Demisexual**: sexual attraction only after deep emotional bonds
- **Demiromantic**: romantic attraction only after deep bonds
- **Gray-asexual / grayromantic**: attraction that's rare, weak, or conditional

## What Ace and Aro People Actually Want

**Not romance or sex — community, friendship, and respect.** This is the part society struggles with most. Ace and aro people build rich lives around:

- Deep friendships that are allowed to be primary
- Queerplatonic partnerships (QPRs) — committed life partnerships without romance or sex
- Chosen families
- Hobbies, passions, and purpose

The cultural assumption that everyone needs a romantic-sexual partner is called *amatonormativity* — and it's the root of most ace/aro erasure.

## The Erasure Problem

Ace and aro people face a specific kind of dismissal:

- "You just haven't found the right person"
- "You're just repressed"
- "The A is for Allies, right?" (it isn't — it's for asexual, aromantic, agender)
- "Ace people don't face discrimination" (they do — acephobia includes "corrective" harassment and even conversion attempts)

## Why Visibility Matters

Young people discovering ace/aro identities need to know the words exist. Many ace people describe years of feeling broken before finding the community. Organizations like AVEN (asexuality.org) and AUREA (aromantic support) exist to make sure no one waits that long.

## How to Be an Ally

1. Believe ace and aro people when they describe their experience.
2. Don't offer fixes, theories, or "the right person."
3. Use the language they give you (QPR, partner, friendship).
4. Push back on jokes and dismissals.
5. Include the A in LGBTQIA+ — visibly.

The A isn't an afterthought. It's a central part of the community, with its own flags, culture, history, and needs. The umbrella is big enough for everyone.
