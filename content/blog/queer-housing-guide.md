# Finding Queer-Friendly Housing and Roommates

Housing is where queer people face some of their most acute safety problems — and where community solutions matter most. Finding a home that's safe, affirming, and affordable is one of the highest-stakes tasks in queer life. Here's how to approach it.

## Why Queer Housing Is Different

- **Safety isn't guaranteed**: Roommates and landlords can be hostile, and housing discrimination is still legal in much of the US (the Fair Housing Act covers sex, and courts have read LGBTQ+ protections into it — but many states lack explicit statutes).
- **Found family is real**: Many queer people choose to live with chosen family — friends, community members, other queers — for mutual support and safety.
- **Trans people face extra barriers**: Deadnaming on leases, bathroom conflicts, and outright refusal of service are common.
- **Homelessness is disproportionate**: Up to 40% of homeless youth are LGBTQ+, driven largely by family rejection.

## The Best Sources for Queer Housing

**Community networks work best:**
- Facebook groups: "[City] LGBTQ+ Housing," "Queer Roommates [City]"
- Local subreddits and Discord servers
- LGBTQ+ community center bulletin boards
- University GSA / queer resource center listings
- Queer-specific housing cooperatives (many cities have them)

**Apps that help:**
- Roomi and SpareRoom let you filter by interests and community
- Bumble BFF can pre-build the social connection before the lease
- Dedicated queer housing platforms are coming — platforms like Umbrella.lgbt will include housing boards

## Vetting Roommates

Ask direct questions — it's normal in the housing world:
- "Are you and your roommates LGBTQ+ or LGBTQ+-friendly?"
- "Is this a sober space?"
- "How do you handle visitors and partners?"
- "Is the lease individual or joint?" (Individual protects you if a roommate leaves)
- For trans people: "Where do people change and shower?"

## Vetting Landlords

- Search the landlord's name and property for complaints.
- Ask for references from current tenants if possible.
- Get everything in writing — including any agreements about names or pronouns.
- Document every interaction: if discrimination happens, records are your evidence.

## Know Your Rights

- **US**: Fair Housing Act protects sexual orientation and gender identity in most circuits. HUD explicitly covers LGBTQ+ people in federally funded housing. State protections vary — check your state.
- **UK**: Equality Act 2010 protects sexual orientation and gender reassignment in housing.
- **Canada/EU/Australia**: Anti-discrimination laws protect LGBTQ+ renters broadly.
- **When it fails**: Contact your local fair housing organization, HUD (US), or equality body. Document everything.

## If You're Facing Homelessness

You are not alone and this is not your fault. Contact:
- **US**: The Trevor Project (866-488-7386), National Runaway Safeline (1-800-786-2929), True Colors United
- **UK**: akt (Albert Kennedy Trust) for LGBTQ+ young people
- **Everywhere**: findahelpline.com

## The Goal

A home where you can be yourself without explanation. That's not a luxury — it's a baseline. Community-built housing networks exist because the market doesn't serve us; use them, and when you can, help the next person find their way in.
