## Short Answer

Gender dysphoria is the distress or discomfort a person feels when their gender identity doesn't match the sex they were assigned at birth. Not every trans or non-binary person experiences dysphoria, and dysphoria can look different for different people.

## What Gender Dysphoria Feels Like

Dysphoria can be physical, social, or both:

- **Physical dysphoria**: Discomfort with your body or specific physical characteristics — feeling like your chest, voice, height, or face doesn't align with who you are.
- **Social dysphoria**: Distress from being seen, referred to, or treated as a gender you are not — being misgendered, deadnamed, or forced into gendered spaces that feel wrong.

It can range from mild unease to severe, debilitating distress. It can be constant or come in waves. There is no single "correct" way to experience dysphoria.

## Do You Need Dysphoria to Be Trans?

No. Some trans and non-binary people experience gender euphoria — the joy of being seen and recognized as their true gender — more prominently than dysphoria. The absence of severe dysphoria does not invalidate your identity.

## What Helps

- **Social transition**: Changing your name, pronouns, clothing, and presentation.
- **Medical transition**: Hormone therapy (HRT), surgeries, or other gender-affirming care — for those who want it.
- **Community**: Connecting with other trans and non-binary people who understand.
- **Therapy**: Working with a trans-affirming therapist can help process dysphoria and navigate transition.

If you're experiencing gender dysphoria, you are not alone, and there are people who want to support you. Check the [resources page](/resources) for helplines and LGBTQ+ organizations near you.
