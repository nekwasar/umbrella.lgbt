## Short Answer

Changing your legal name and gender marker is a multi-step process that varies by country and state — but the core steps are: 1) decide your name, 2) file for a legal name change, 3) update your gender marker, 4) update your documents (ID, passport, bank, insurance).

## Step 1: Choose Your Name

- Legally, most places allow any name change as long as it's not for fraud.
- Some places require public notice in a newspaper; many courts allow "good cause" exceptions, especially for trans people (safety). Ask about waivers.

## Step 2: Legal Name Change

**US (state-dependent)**:
- File a petition with your county court (fees $100–$400; many states allow fee waivers).
- Some states require a court hearing; many are paper-only.
- After approval, you get a court order — the key document for everything else.

**UK**:
- Deed poll (free) or statutory declaration. Simple and informal.

**Other countries**: Varies widely — some require medical documentation, others (Germany's SBGG) now allow simple self-declaration.

## Step 3: Gender Marker

- **US**: Passport gender markers can be updated to X (or M/F) with a doctor's letter or self-attestation depending on the administration. State IDs vary — some states allow X, some don't allow any change.
- **UK**: Gender Recognition Certificate requires a medical panel process; however, passports and driving licenses can be updated with a letter from a doctor or specialist.
- **X markers**: A growing number of countries and US states now offer X gender markers on IDs.

## Step 4: Update Everything

Order matters: court order / deed poll first, then passport, then driver's license, then bank, insurance, payroll, school records, medical records, and subscriptions. Make a checklist and work through it.
