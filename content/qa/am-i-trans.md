## Short Answer

There's no test for being trans — if the label fits your experience, it's yours. Common signs include persistent discomfort with your assigned gender, gender euphoria when seen differently, and a recurring wish to be another gender.

## Common Signs People Report

- **A long history of discomfort**: Unease with your name, pronouns, body, or the gendered expectations placed on you — sometimes from early childhood, sometimes surfacing later.
- **Gender euphoria**: Deep joy when treated as another gender (see our euphoria guide). For many people, euphoria is the stronger signal than dysphoria.
- **Recurring wishes**: "I wish I were a boy/girl" said to yourself for years — wishing isn't just wondering.
- **Feeling fake**: Playing a role, performing a gender, waiting to "feel like a real man/woman."
- **Disconnect from your body**: Feeling like your body isn't yours, avoiding mirrors, or zoning out around gendered body parts.
- **Relief in imagination**: Feeling more yourself in stories, games, or characters of another gender.

None of these alone prove you're trans — but if you're reading this list and feeling seen, that's worth taking seriously.

## The Questions That Help

Instead of "Am I really trans?", ask:

- "If I could press a button to wake up as the other gender — no consequences — would I?"
- "Do I want to be *seen as* another gender by strangers?"
- "Does calling myself trans make my life feel more honest?"
- "Am I searching for proof because I'm not trans — or because I'm scared?"

## What's Not Required

- You don't need severe dysphoria to be trans.
- You don't need to have "always known."
- You don't need to want hormones or surgery.
- You don't need to pass.
- You don't need certainty — questioning is part of the process, not a sign you're wrong.

## How to Explore Safely

- Try new names and pronouns in anonymous or trusted spaces
- Read trans experiences (memoirs, communities, our glossary)
- Talk to a trans-affirming therapist (see our therapist guide)
- Connect with trans communities — being witnessed by people who get it changes everything

## Remember

Trans is not a verdict you're waiting for — it's a label you're allowed to try on. If it helps you understand yourself and live more fully, it's working.
