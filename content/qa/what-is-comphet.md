## Short Answer

Compulsory heterosexuality ("comphet") is the societal pressure that assumes everyone is straight and that straight relationships are the only valid path. It can make queer people date the "expected" gender for years before realizing who they actually love.

## Where It Comes From

- Almost every story, movie, and song centers straight couples
- Families assume children will be straight and ask about "boyfriends/girlfriends"
- Schools and media rarely present same-gender love as an option
- Religion, law, and culture have historically treated straightness as the only acceptable path

The term comes from Adrienne Rich's 1980 essay "Compulsory Heterosexuality and Lesbian Existence," which argued that heterosexuality is enforced by society, not chosen naturally.

## How Comphet Shows Up

- Dating the opposite gender "because that's what you do"
- Enjoying the *idea* of a relationship without real attraction
- Wondering why things feel "off" or performative
- Feeling relief when a straight relationship ends
- Never considering same-gender attraction because it was never presented as an option
- Assuming you're "defective" rather than queer

## The Realization

Many people describe comphet as a fog lifting: the moment they realize attraction to the same gender was possible all along, years of confusion suddenly make sense. This is common and normal — for lesbians, bi people, gay men, and everyone else.

## If You're Questioning

- You don't need proof to be queer. "I might be" is enough to explore.
- Reading about comphet helps: *The CompHet Experiment* (a well-known tumblr/essay), discussions in sapphic spaces, and community threads.
- Experiment safely: online spaces, trusted friends, new communities.
- There's no deadline. Realizing late is common — at 20, 30, 40, or 60.
