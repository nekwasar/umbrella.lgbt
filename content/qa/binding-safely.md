## Short Answer

Bind safely by using a purpose-made chest binder, wearing it for no more than 8 hours a day, never sleeping in it, never exercising in it, and never using tape or bandages meant for other purposes.

## The Golden Rules

1. **Use a real binder** — GC2B, Underworks, Spectrum Outfitters (UK), or other reputable brands. Measure carefully and size up if between sizes.
2. **8 hours max** — longer risks rib and lung damage. When you hit the limit, take it off.
3. **Never sleep in it** — your body needs to expand and recover.
4. **Never exercise in it** — running, lifting, or cardio in a binder can cause serious breathing problems. Use looser compression or sports bras for exercise.
5. **No household tape, no ace bandages, no duct tape** — these don't expand with breathing and can crack ribs and cause permanent damage.

## Warning Signs to Stop

- Pain in ribs, back, or chest
- Shortness of breath or dizziness
- Skin irritation, rashes, or open sores
- Coughing or chest tightness

If any of these happen: remove the binder immediately, rest, and see a doctor if symptoms persist.

## Practical Tips

- **Start slow**: Build up wearing time gradually over 1-2 weeks.
- **Listen to your body**: Everyone's ribs are different. Some people can't manage 8 hours; that's fine.
- **Skin care**: Wash the binder regularly, moisturize, and give your skin breaks.
- **Brake days**: Take days off when you can — home is a good time to be unbound.

## Alternatives

- Loose-fitting clothing, layering, and sports bras can work for mild needs.
- Top surgery is the permanent option for people who want it. Binders should never be a permanent necessity.
