## Short Answer

Ballroom culture is a Black and Latinx queer subculture built around competitive "balls," "houses" (chosen families), and performance categories like vogue, runway, and realness. It emerged in Harlem in the late 20th century and gave the world vogue dance.

## The Balls

Balls are competitions where participants "walk" categories judged on performance, style, and authenticity. Classic categories include:

- **Vogue**: A dance form with angular, catwalk-inspired poses and choreography
- **Runway**: Fashion-catwalk performance
- **Realness**: Passing as a straight, cisgender person — a category born from survival, walking into spaces where being visibly queer was dangerous
- **Face**: Beauty and facial expression
- **Bizarre**: Extreme, creative, often gender-bending looks

## Houses and Chosen Family

Ballroom is organized into houses — families led by mothers and fathers who mentor, protect, and care for younger members, many of whom were rejected by their blood families for being queer or trans. Legendary houses include LaBeija, Xtravaganza, Ninja, and Mugler. House mothers and fathers are real parents: feeding, housing, and nurturing their children.

## The History

Ballroom emerged in 1960s-70s Harlem as a response to the exclusion of Black and Latinx queer people from white gay spaces. It flourished through the 80s and 90s, documented in Jennie Livingston's landmark 1990 documentary *Paris Is Burning*.

## Legacy

- Madonna's "Vogue" (1990) brought the dance global — though much credit belongs to the Black and Latinx performers who created it.
- *Pose* (2018-2021) brought ballroom to television.
- Stars like Leiomy Maldonado and Dominique Jackson are celebrated icons.
- Ballroom remains a vital protective space for Black and Latinx trans and queer people — including trans sex workers who helped pioneer the culture.

## How to Respect It

Ballroom is Black and Latinx culture. Show up as a guest, not a colonizer: learn, tip performers, credit the culture's origins, and support its community organizations.
