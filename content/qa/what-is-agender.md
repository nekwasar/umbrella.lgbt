## Short Answer

Agender means having no gender — or a gender identity that's entirely outside the concept of gender. Agender people may feel genderless, gender-neutral, or simply that "gender" isn't a category that applies to them.

## What Agender Feels Like

There's no single experience, but common descriptions include:

- "I don't feel like a man or a woman — the categories just don't apply"
- "I don't have an internal sense of gender at all"
- "Gender feels like a language I don't speak"
- "I'm a person who happens to have a body"

Agender is distinct from being indecisive or "not caring about gender roles." It's an internal experience of genderlessness — like being colorblind to a spectrum everyone else sees.

## Agender vs. Related Labels

- **Non-binary**: umbrella term — agender is under it
- **Genderfluid**: gender changes over time (agender doesn't)
- **Bigender**: two genders (agender: zero)
- **Cisgender**: gender matches assigned sex (agender: no gender to match)

Many agender people identify as trans; some don't. Both are valid.

## Pronouns and Presentation

- Agender people use they/them, he/him, she/her, neopronouns, or any combination — whatever fits.
- Presentation varies wildly: some dress androgynously, some don't. There's no "agender look."
- You cannot tell someone is agender by looking at them.

## Common Experiences

- Being confused by gendered language aimed at you ("young lady," "handsome man")
- Feeling neutral or "nothing" when asked about your gender
- Discomfort (or indifference) with gendered spaces — bathrooms, changing rooms, uniforms
- Finding community in non-binary and trans spaces

## If You Think You Might Be Agender

- Try the label in your head, in anonymous spaces, or with trusted friends
- Read agender experiences (r/agender, agender.org resources)
- Remember: labels are tools. If "agender" makes your experience more explainable — to yourself or others — it's working.
