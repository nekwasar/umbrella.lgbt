## Short Answer

Find an LGBTQ+ affirming therapist using specialized directories (TherapyDen, Inclusive Therapists, Psychology Today with filters), asking the right questions in a consultation call, and never settling for a provider who questions your identity.

## Where to Search

- **TherapyDen**: Built specifically to list LGBTQ+ affirming therapists
- **Inclusive Therapists**: Centers marginalized identities, including BIPOC and disabled queer folks
- **Psychology Today**: Filter by "LGBTQ+" specialty and sexual orientation/gender identity issues
- **Open Path Collective**: Low-cost therapy ($30-70/session), filterable
- **Local LGBTQ+ centers**: Almost all maintain trusted provider lists
- **Referrals**: Ask your queer community — word of mouth is the most reliable source

## Questions to Ask in the First Call

1. "Have you worked with queer/trans clients before? How often?"
2. "What's your approach to gender identity?" (You want explicit affirmation, not "exploration")
3. "Do you have training in trans-specific care?" (WPATH standards, informed consent familiarity)
4. "How do you handle a client questioning their identity?"
5. "Are you comfortable with non-traditional relationship structures?" (if relevant — poly, QPRs)

A good therapist answers these directly. Hesitation or defensiveness is a red flag.

## Red Flags

- "Let's explore why you feel you're trans" (as if it's a problem)
- "Are you sure?" on identity
- Recommending conversion-type or "wait and see" approaches for identity
- Assuming you want to talk about being queer when you're there for anxiety
- Making you educate them session after session

## Specialized Care

- **Trans-specific therapy**: Look for therapists experienced with transition pathways, letters for surgery if needed, and dysphoria support.
- **Ace/aro-affirming therapy**: Rarer — check ace-focused directories (AVEN's community recommendations) and ask directly.
- **Queer couples therapy**: Different modality — seek therapists listed for LGBTQ+ couples.
- **Group therapy**: Many centers run queer support groups — often free and highly effective.

## Cost Barriers

- Open Path Collective (sliding scale)
- Many therapists offer "sliding scale" — ask, it's normal
- University training clinics (low-cost, supervised students)
- Employee assistance programs (EAP) — often free sessions
- Some US states fund LGBTQ+ mental health services (check your center)
