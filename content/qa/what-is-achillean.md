## Short Answer

Achillean describes men and non-binary people who love men — covering gay men, bisexual men, pansexual men, and anyone whose attraction to men is central. The term comes from Achilles, the Greek hero of *The Iliad*.

## Who Is Achillean?

- Gay men
- Bisexual men who love men
- Pansexual men
- Non-binary people connected to masculinity who love men
- Queer men who don't use narrower labels

It parallels "sapphic" as an umbrella term for same-gender attraction centered on men.

## The Origin

Achilles and Patroclus are the most famous male couple in Western literature — their bond in *The Iliad* (circa 800 BCE) has been read as romantic love for over 2,000 years. Madeline Miller's *The Song of Achilles* (2011) made the pairing iconic for a new generation.

## Why Use It?

- **Inclusivity**: Includes bi, pan, and queer men who don't fit "gay" alone.
- **Reframes the focus**: "Gay" often reads as identity; "achillean" centers *attraction to men*, which fits more people.
- **Ancient roots**: Using it connects modern mlm (men loving men) experience to a deep historical tradition.

## Achillean Culture

- Achillean literature (from Plato's *Symposium* to *The Song of Achilles*)
- mlm romance and fanfiction spaces (r/MM_RomanceBooks, AO3)
- Queer men's community spaces — book clubs, sports leagues, and (eventually) platforms like Umbrella.lgbt

If you're a man or masc-leaning person who loves men and want a word that holds the whole picture, achillean is yours.
