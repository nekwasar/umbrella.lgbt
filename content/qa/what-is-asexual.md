## Short Answer

Asexuality is a sexual orientation where a person experiences little or no sexual attraction to other people. It's not a disorder, a phase, or a result of trauma — it's a valid identity that describes how someone experiences (or doesn't experience) attraction.

## What Asexuality Is

Asexual people (often called "aces") don't experience sexual attraction, or experience it only rarely or under specific circumstances. This does not mean:
- They can't fall in love (romantic attraction is separate)
- They can't have relationships (many ace people are in happy partnerships)
- They can't have sex (some ace people have sex for various reasons)
- They are "broken" or "missing something"

## The Ace Spectrum

Asexuality exists on a spectrum:

- **Asexual**: Little or no sexual attraction.
- **Demisexual**: Only experiences sexual attraction after forming a deep emotional connection.
- **Gray-asexual**: Experiences sexual attraction rarely, weakly, or only under specific circumstances.

## Asexuality vs. Aromanticism

Sexual attraction and romantic attraction are different:
- **Asexual**: Little or no *sexual* attraction.
- **Aromantic**: Little or no *romantic* attraction.

A person can be both asexual and aromantic, or asexual but romantic, or aromantic but sexual. These are distinct identities.

## Common Misconceptions

- "You just haven't met the right person yet" — No. This is like telling a gay person they haven't met the right opposite-gender person.
- "It's just a phase" — For some people, identity evolves, but that doesn't make current identity less real.
- "Asexuals can't love" — Romantic love, emotional intimacy, and deep connection are all separate from sexual attraction.
