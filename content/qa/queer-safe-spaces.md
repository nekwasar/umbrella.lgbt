## Short Answer

Safe queer spaces are places where you can be openly yourself without fear — found through LGBTQ+ community centers, queer-owned businesses, community groups, and increasingly, online spaces designed with safety in mind.

## Types of Safe Spaces

- **LGBTQ+ community centers**: The gold standard — staffed, resourced, and vetted.
- **Queer-owned bars and cafes**: Marked by pride flags, inclusive staff, and queer community programming.
- **Community groups**: Sports leagues (Stonewall Sports), book clubs, hiking groups, and support groups.
- **Online spaces**: Discord servers with real moderation, forums, and (coming soon) community platforms built for queer safety.
- **Allies' spaces**: Some organizations actively mark themselves as safe with inclusive policies and signage.

## How to Vet a Space

- **Flags and signage**: Visible pride symbols signal active welcome.
- **Ask the community**: Local queer Facebook groups and subreddits know which venues are genuinely safe.
- **Watch for "rainbow-washing"**: A pride flag in the window means little if staff and policies aren't actually inclusive. Trust word of mouth over decor.
- **Check policies**: Gendered bathroom policies, staff training, and anti-harassment rules matter more than decor.

## If There's No Safe Space Near You

- **Create one**: Meetup groups, small events, and online communities can start small — even a monthly coffee meetup.
- **Use online spaces**: Real community can exist entirely online, especially in rural or hostile areas.
- **Know your fallbacks**: Libraries, some places of worship, and universities often host inclusive groups.

## Digital Safety

In online spaces, look for: active moderation, clear community rules, verified-age features, anonymous/closeted options, and no data-selling. The platform we're building — Umbrella.lgbt — is designed around exactly these principles.
