# PrEP and HIV Prevention: What You Need to Know

HIV prevention has been transformed in the last decade — and a huge part of that transformation is PrEP. If you've been putting off learning about it, this guide is for you. It's simpler than you think.

## The Basics

**PrEP** (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis) is medication that prevents HIV before exposure. Take it consistently, and the risk of getting HIV from sex drops by about 99%. It's one of the most effective preventive measures in modern medicine.

Two main forms:
- **Daily pill** (Truvada, Descovy, generics): one pill a day, simple and proven
- **Injectable** (Apretude): every two months, increasingly popular

## Who Should Take It

If you answer yes to any of these, PrEP is worth discussing with a provider:

- You're a man who has sex with men and don't always use condoms
- You have a partner living with HIV (even an undetectable one — extra layer)
- You've had an STI in the last year
- You don't know your partner's HIV status and don't always use condoms
- You inject drugs or share equipment

PrEP isn't a judgment on your behavior — it's a tool for people who want protection that works even when condoms don't.

## How to Get It

1. **Ask any doctor or clinic.** PrEP is a standard prescription.
2. **LGBTQ+ health centers and Planned Parenthood** offer it on informed consent — no therapist letters needed.
3. **Telehealth** (US): Folx Health, Plume, Q Care Plus — all online, discreet.
4. **UK**: Free via NHS sexual health clinics (like 56 Dean Street in London).
5. **Many places subsidize it**: in the US, most insurance covers it, and patient assistance programs exist for the uninsured. In much of Europe, it's free.

## The Routine

- HIV test before starting, then every 3 months while on it (standard clinic practice)
- Kidney function monitoring (simple blood test) — usually yearly
- Most people have zero or mild side effects (initial nausea passes within weeks)

## What PrEP Doesn't Do

- It doesn't prevent other STIs — syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia are still a real risk. Regular STI testing is still essential.
- It doesn't prevent pregnancy.
- It doesn't work if you don't take it — consistency is everything.

## The Other Half of the Story: U=U

PrEP protects HIV-negative people. For HIV-positive people, the revolution is U=U: **Undetectable = Untransmittable.** A person living with HIV on effective treatment — with an undetectable viral load — cannot pass the virus to sexual partners. Zero transmissions across thousands of studied couples.

This changes everything: HIV-positive people on treatment are not a threat, and their status alone is no reason for fear or rejection.

## The Stigma Problem

HIV stigma still causes real harm: people avoid testing, avoid treatment, and avoid relationships out of fear. The truth is that HIV today — caught early and treated — is a manageable condition. People with HIV live full, long, healthy lives, and they cannot transmit the virus when undetectable.

## The Bottom Line

- Get tested regularly — it's normal, smart, and usually free
- If you're eligible, consider PrEP — it's safe, effective, and widely available
- If you're living with HIV, get treated — undetectable means healthy and safe
- Treat people living with HIV with dignity, not fear

Prevention is community care. Every person on PrEP or treatment is a link in a chain that ends the epidemic. Be a link.
