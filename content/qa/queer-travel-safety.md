## Short Answer

Queer travel safety is about research, preparation, and discretion. Before you travel: check the legal status of LGBTQ+ people in your destination, research safe areas, plan your accommodations, and prepare a safety plan — including knowing when to stay closeted.

## Step 1: Research Your Destination

- **Legal status**: Check ILGA's world map (ilga.org) for laws on same-sex relationships, trans rights, and protections. Over 60 countries still criminalize queer identity — in some, it's punishable by death.
- **Recent news**: Laws change. Search "[country] LGBTQ+ 2026" before you go.
- **Visas and documents**: If your passport gender marker doesn't match your presentation, research entry rules for that country carefully.

## Step 2: Where to Stay

- Book **queer-friendly hotels** (many are listed on services like misterb, or in city guides) — staff who support you matter when you need help.
- **Staying with locals**: Homestay hosts who are queer or allies are safer than strangers.
- In hostile countries, **discreet booking** (generic hotels, known international chains) can be safer than boutique queer spaces.

## Step 3: How to Behave

- **Gauge the scene on arrival**: In queer-friendly countries, be yourself; in hostile ones, adapt. This isn't cowardice — it's strategy.
- **Hand-holding, PDA, and dress**: Research local norms. What's fine in Berlin may be dangerous in Cairo.
- **Use local knowledge**: Local queer people (via online communities) know which areas and apps are safe.
- **Apps with safety features**: Use apps that allow hiding distance, incognito mode, and discreet icons (many queer apps offer these).

## Step 4: Your Safety Plan

- Share your itinerary with a trusted person at home
- Save embassy/consulate numbers
- Download offline maps
- Have a "cover story" for border questioning if needed
- Know the emergency numbers (112 in EU, 911 in US, 100 in many Asian countries)

## Resources

- **Equaldex**: Crowdsourced LGBTQ+ legal and cultural data by country
