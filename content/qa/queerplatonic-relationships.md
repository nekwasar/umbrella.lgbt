## Short Answer

A queerplatonic relationship (QPR) is a committed partnership that is more than friendship but doesn't fit romantic or sexual categories. Think: life partner, but without the expectation of romance or sex.

## What It Looks Like

- Living together, sharing finances, raising pets or children
- Being each other's primary emotional support and default "person"
- Making life plans together — aging, moves, big decisions
- Physical closeness like cuddling, hand-holding, or even sex — negotiated between the partners, not assumed

The key difference from friendship: it's a *chosen partnership* with explicit commitments and boundaries, like a relationship — just not a romantic one.

## Who Has QPRs

- **Aromantic and asexual people** — the identity is central to ace/aro communities, where romance and sex aren't assumed parts of partnership.
- **Anyone** who finds that their most important relationship doesn't fit "friend" or "romantic partner."

## Why It Matters

Society treats romance as the only path to committed partnership (a bias called amatonormativity). QPRs prove that deep, committed, life-shaping love doesn't require romance. They also challenge the idea that relationships need to be ranked — partners, best friends, and QPR partners can all be equally important.

## How to Navigate One

- **Communicate explicitly**: Define what your QPR includes — commitment level, boundaries, physical touch, future plans.
- **Negotiate with family and society**: Expect people to misunderstand; decide together how to explain it.
- **It's real**: A QPR is not "settling" or "friends with extra steps." It's its own category of relationship.
