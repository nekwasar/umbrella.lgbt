## Short Answer

If your family rejects you for being queer, protect yourself first, build your support network, and know that rejection is a reflection of their limitations — not your worth. There are organizations and communities ready to catch you.

## Immediate Steps

1. **Safety first**: If you're at risk of being thrown out, find safe housing before any big conversations. LGBTQ+ youth shelters and orgs like the Ali Forney Center (NYC), the Albert Kennedy Trust (UK), and True Colors United exist for exactly this.
2. **Reach out immediately**: Call or text a helpline. The Trevor Project (866-488-7386, text START to 678-678), Trans Lifeline (877-565-8860). Talking to someone who gets it changes everything.
3. **Connect with community**: Local LGBTQ+ centers, school GSAs, online queer spaces, and (soon) platforms like Umbrella.lgbt.

## Build Your Found Family

Rejection from blood family doesn't mean you'll be alone. Found family — chosen people who love you unconditionally — is a deep tradition in queer communities:

- Join queer support groups and social communities
- Find mentors: older queer people who've been through this
- Let your friends in: you don't have to process this alone
- Create traditions with your people: holidays, birthdays, everyday rituals

## Practical Survival

- **Finances**: If you're cut off, look into independent living programs, queer-friendly jobs, and community support funds. Many LGBTQ+ orgs run emergency funds for exactly this situation.
- **Documents**: Get copies of your ID, birth certificate, and medical records before any relationship breaks down.
- **Housing**: LGBTQ+ youth housing programs, roommates from community networks, and safe-house networks.

## The Long Game

- Some families come around after months or years. Some never do. Both outcomes are survivable.
- You are allowed to grieve the family you deserved — and to build a better one.
- You are not "too much." You are not a burden. The failure here is theirs.
